# Source hygiene for legal citations

Legal statements in code comments, docs and replies follow the same evidence rule as library claims
(`evidence-before-claims`), with stricter sources.

## Primary sources

| What | Where |
|---|---|
| EU regulations and directives (GDPR, DSA) | EUR-Lex, https://eur-lex.europa.eu |
| German federal statutes (UWG, TDDDG, UStG, AO, BGB) | https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de |
| EDPB guidelines and endorsed Article 29 Working Party papers | https://www.edpb.europa.eu |
| Adequacy decisions | the European Commission's adequacy page |
| Court decisions | the court's own publication; for the CJEU, https://curia.europa.eu |

Commentary sites and law-firm articles are useful for orientation. Cite the primary text.

## Rules

- Cite article, paragraph and point ("Art. 30(5)", "§ 7 Abs. 3 Nr. 4 UWG"), not just the law.
- Write the date you fetched the text next to the citation.
- Quote short phrases exactly; paraphrase the rest and say so.
- Never cite a court decision, case number or authority guidance from memory. If you cannot fetch it, say that
  you could not, and mark the statement Unverified.
- Statutes are renamed and renumbered. The German device-storage law is now the TDDDG, while its URL on
  gesetze-im-internet.de still reads `ttdsg`. Check the current title on the page.
- Lists change: adequacy decisions, authority DSFA lists, and subprocessor terms. Check them on the day you
  decide, not from an earlier note.
- Every legal draft carries "review-ready draft, not legal advice", its scope (EU, DE) and its sources with dates.
