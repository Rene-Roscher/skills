## Choosing a channel

Evidence for where the buyer looks, strongest first:

1. What paying customers answered in the sign-up source field or in interviews.
2. Search demand for the problem in the buyer's language (`search-measurement`).
3. Communities, newsletters, marketplaces and events the buyer reads or attends, named by customers.
4. Where competitors get customers (`market-analysis`): a hint, not proof.

Typical B2B SaaS channels: organic search and comparison pages (`search-visibility`), integrations and app
marketplaces of tools the buyer already uses, partner and reseller channels, communities and expert content,
events, paid search on high-intent queries, outbound where legal (see the outreach reference). Paid social and
broad display rarely fit small B2B budgets; prove them like any other channel.

## Channel plan row

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Channel | integration listing in a project tool's app marketplace |
| Evidence | 4 of 10 interviewed customers found tools there |
| Target cost per paying customer | from the pricing model's payback target |
| Spend and hours | listing work, 6 hours a month upkeep |
| Attribution | `source` parameter on the listing link, stored on the account (`search-measurement`) |
| Read date and cohorts | two monthly cohorts after go-live |
| Stop / keep / scale rule | written before start |

## Launch checklist

- [ ] Positioning and price stored; launch message checked against the product-truth table.
- [ ] A new account reaches its first result without help; tested on a fresh account.
- [ ] Attribution on every launch link; the sign-up source stored on the account.
- [ ] Directory and marketplace profiles complete and current.
- [ ] Owned audience contacted only with consent or under § 7 Abs. 3 UWG.
- [ ] Support capacity and a known-issues note for launch day.
- [ ] Changelog and docs updated; pages for new features live and indexed (`search-visibility`).
- [ ] A read date and the metrics to report (attributed sign-ups, paying customers, cost per paying customer).

One launch moment fits a new product, a new plan or a feature buyers asked for. Smaller releases go out
continuously through the changelog, in-product notes and the owned audience.

## Directories and marketplaces

- Fill every field with the same facts as the site: description sentence, categories, prices, limits,
  integrations, screenshots of the current product, legal entity and Impressum link.
- Write the entry yourself; directories often reject generated entries.
- Keep a list: profile URL, last checked, owner. Re-check after every pricing or major feature change; outdated
  third-party profiles are what assistants repeat.

## Review platforms

| Platform | Incentives | Selective invitations | Source (verified 2026-11-03) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trustpilot | never in connection with writing or editing a review | not allowed ("must not cherry pick") | https://corporate.trustpilot.com/legal/for-everyone/action-we-take |
| Google (Maps, Business Profile) | not allowed | not allowed | https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114 |
| G2 | G2's own campaigns, never based on the review's opinion; vendor incentives must be disclosed to G2 | segmenting out negative reviews not allowed | https://legal.g2.com/community-guidelines |
| Others (Capterra, OMR Reviews and similar) | read the platform's current rules first | | Unverified |

German law on top (review-ready draft, not legal advice):

- Fake or commissioned reviews and misrepresented reviews are always unfair (UWG Anhang Nr. 23b, 23c).
- If you show reviews, say whether and how you check they come from real customers (UWG § 5b Abs. 3).
- A review incentive that the review does not disclose can hide a commercial purpose (UWG § 5a Abs. 4).
- A review request by email can be advertising under § 7 UWG.

Sources: https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/uwg_2004/anhang.html,
https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/uwg_2004/__5b.html, https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/uwg_2004/__5a.html
(verified 2026-11-03).

## Community and press

- In communities, answer the question asked; mention the product where it is the honest answer and say you
  built it.
- Press and newsletters take data: a finding from aggregated product data or a survey, with the method stated.
- Measure both by attributed paying customers, not mentions.
