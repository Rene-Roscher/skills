# Citing issues, CVEs, RFCs, statutes and rulings

Load before you cite an issue, CVE, RFC, statute or ruling.

The rule for all of them: cite only what you opened in this session, with the URL on the same line. A
citation that does not resolve makes the claim that depends on it unverified.

## Issues and pull requests

- Open the URL. Check that the title and state match what you claim.
- A repository may have issues disabled. Then there is no issue to cite.
- A claim that a release contains a fix names that release or tag, not only the merged pull request.

## CVEs and advisories

- Open the CVE record or the vendor advisory and check the affected version range against the lockfile.
- A CVE ID you remember is a lead, not a citation.

## RFCs and specifications

- Cite the document, the section number and the URL of the published text.
- Check whether a later document updates or obsoletes it.

## Statutes and regulations (DE, EU)

- Name the jurisdiction and the retrieval date.
- Germany: gesetze-im-internet.de publishes consolidated federal law and states that these texts are not the
  official version. The official text is the Bundesgesetzblatt, published electronically on recht.bund.de
  since 1 January 2023 (both stated on https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/, verified 2026-10).
- EU: cite the act on EUR-Lex with its URL.
- German legal terms without an exact English equivalent stay German, with English in parentheses: AVV (data
  processing agreement), VVT (record of processing activities), TOM (technical and organisational measures),
  Impressum (legal notice).
- Mark the output "review-ready draft, not legal or tax advice".
- Data protection and other legal depth belongs to the `gdpr-engineering` skill.

## Rulings

Court, date and docket number, with a URL to the decision text. Without all three, no citation.
